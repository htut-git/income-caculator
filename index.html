Comparing the 5 Global Budgeting Frameworks

Effective income management is never one-size-fits-all. A salaried employee in Singapore, a small business owner in Yangon, and a freelancer in London face very different cash-flow patterns. By pairing timeless ethical principles with practical modern percentage rules, you can choose a framework that fits your current season of life and switch seamlessly as your goals evolve.

1. Buddhist Method (25/50/25)

Rooted in the Sigālovāda Sutta: 25% for living and generosity (dāna), 50% reinvested into work or business growth, and 25% locked in reserve.

How the Percentage Formulas Work

Percentage-based budgeting eliminates guesswork by scaling automatically with your paycheck. Instead of inventing rigid dollar caps every month, you multiply your net monthly income (after taxes) by each category's target percentage:

Category Allocation = Monthly Net Income × (Category Percentage ÷ 100)

Using percentages ensures that whenever your income rises, your savings, emergency reserves, and generosity grow proportionally rather than disappearing into lifestyle inflation. And if you experience an irregular month, the ratios keep your priorities intact without forcing a complicated spreadsheet overhaul.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I enter my gross income or net (take-home) income?

Always use your net monthly income—the amount that actually lands in your bank account after taxes, social contributions, and mandatory payroll deductions. Budgeting from take-home pay ensures your percentage buckets match real spendable cash.

Which budgeting method should a beginner start with?

If you want the simplest habit possible, start with the 80/20 Rule (automate 20% into savings on payday and live on 80%). Once you are comfortable tracking essentials versus lifestyle spending, transition to the 50/30/20 Rule or the Buddhist 25/50/25 Method if you run a business or invest heavily in your skills.

Is my income data saved or sent to any server?

No. Every calculation runs 100% locally inside your web browser using client-side JavaScript. Your income numbers and currency selections are never uploaded, stored, or shared with BalanceLedger.
